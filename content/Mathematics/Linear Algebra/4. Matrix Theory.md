
Keep in mind: A matrix, and its corresponding operations are defined such that a single matrix is capable of representing a linear map.

### Matrices

- **Definition of a Matrix**: An $m$-by-$n$ matrix $A$ is a rectangular array of elements of $\mathbf{F}$ with $m$ rows and $n$ columns. The notation $A_{j,k}$ denotes the entry in row $j$ and column $k$.
- **Matrix of a Linear Map**: Suppose $T \in \mathcal{L}(V, W)$ with $v_1, \dots, v_n$ as a basis for $V$ and $w_1, \dots, w_m$ as a basis for $W$. The matrix of $T$ with respect to these bases, denoted $\mathcal{M}(T)$, is the $m$-by-$n$ matrix whose entries $A_{j,k}$ satisfy the equation $Tv_k = \sum_{j=1}^m A_{j,k}w_j$.
	- To construct a matrix from a linear map, we apply $T$ to each vector in a basis $v_1, \dots, v_n$ of $V$ and write the resulting vector $Tv_{k} \in W$ as a linear combination of the basis $w_1, \dots, w_m$ of $W$. 

### Matrix Operations

- **Matrix Addition**: The sum of two matrices of the same size is the matrix obtained by adding corresponding entries: $(A+C)_{j,k} = A_{j,k} + C_{j,k}$.
	- For $S, T \in \mathcal{L}(V, W)$, the matrix of the sum is the sum of the matrices: $\mathcal{M}(S+T) = \mathcal{M}(S) + \mathcal{M}(T)$.
- **Matrix Scalar Multiplication**: The product of a scalar $\lambda$ and a matrix $A$ is obtained by multiplying every entry in the matrix by that scalar: $(\lambda A)_{j,k} = \lambda A_{j,k}$
	- For $\lambda \in \mathbf{F}$ and $T \in \mathcal{L}(V, W)$, the matrix of the scalar multiplication is the scalar multiple of the matrix $\mathcal{M}(\lambda T) = \lambda \mathcal{M}(T)$.
- **Matrix Multiplication**: If $A$ is an $m$-by-$n$ matrix and $B$ is an $n$-by-$p$ matrix, then $AB$ is the $m$-by-$p$ matrix whose entry in row $j$, column $k$ is given by $(AB)_{j,k} = \sum_{r=1}^n A_{j,r}B_{r,k}$.
	- For $T \in \mathcal{L}(U, V)$ and $S \in \mathcal{L}(V, W)$, then the matrix of the composition $ST$ is the product of the individual matrices: $\mathcal{M}(ST) = \mathcal{M}(S)\mathcal{M}(T)$.

### Perspectives on Multiplication

- The entry $(AB)_{j,k}$ equals row $j$ of $A$ times column $k$ of $B$.
- Column $k$ of the product $AB$ can be found by multiplying the matrix $A$ by the $k^{\text{th}}$ column of $B$.
- $Ab$ is a linear combination of the columns of $A$, where the scalars are the entries of the $n$-by-1 matrix $b$.
- A similar perspective holds for the rows of $AB$.
- Column $k$ of the product $AB$ is a linear combination of the columns of $A$ with coefficients from the $k^{\text{th}}$ column of $B$. Row $j$ of the product $AB$ is a linear combination of the rows of $B$ with coefficients from the $j_{\text{th}}$ row from $A$.
