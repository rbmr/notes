### Preliminary Knowledge

This note assumes familiarity with entropy ([[1. Entropy and Information Measures]]), source coding ([[4. Source Coding]]), and differential entropy of the Gaussian ([[6. Differential Entropy and Gaussian Channels]]).

### Lossy Compression

- Source coding ([[4. Source Coding]]) asks how few bits are needed to represent a source *exactly*. **Rate-distortion theory** asks the more forgiving question: how few bits are needed to represent a source *approximately*, up to some tolerated distortion — the setting for essentially all real compression of images, audio, and video, where exact reconstruction is unnecessary and often impossible at any reasonable rate.
- A **distortion measure** $d(x,\hat x)\ge 0$ quantifies the cost of reconstructing source value $x$ as $\hat x$ (with $d(x,x)=0$). The most common choice for real-valued sources is **squared-error distortion**, $d(x,\hat x) = (x-\hat x)^2$.
- A **rate-distortion code** of rate $R$ maps each source symbol (or block of symbols) to one of $2^{nR}$ reconstruction values; its distortion is the expected value $E[d(X,\hat X)]$ under the true source distribution.

### The Rate-Distortion Function

- The **rate-distortion function** $R(D)$ is the smallest rate achievable while keeping expected distortion at or below $D$:
	$$
	R(D) = \min_{p(\hat x\mid x)\,:\,E[d(X,\hat X)]\le D} I(X;\hat X)
	$$
	the minimization ranging over conditional distributions $p(\hat x\mid x)$ (a "test channel" from source to reconstruction) satisfying the distortion budget. This mirrors channel capacity's $\max_{p(x)} I(X;Y)$ almost exactly, but with the roles reversed: capacity *maximizes* mutual information subject to a constraint on the input marginal, while $R(D)$ *minimizes* mutual information subject to a constraint on the joint (via the distortion). Since $I(X;\hat X)$ is a convex function of $p(\hat x\mid x)$ for fixed $p(x)$ (the dual fact to the concavity used in [[5. Channel Capacity]]), and the distortion constraint is linear in $p(\hat x\mid x)$, this minimization is also a genuine convex optimization problem.
- **Rate-Distortion Theorem** (stated without proof — like the noisy-channel coding theorem, the proof is a random-coding argument built on the joint AEP): $R(D)$ is exactly the minimum rate at which a source can be compressed and reconstructed with expected distortion at most $D$. No code of rate below $R(D)$ can achieve distortion $D$, and codes of rate arbitrarily close to $R(D)$ (from above) exist that do.
- Boundary cases: $R(0)$ recovers ordinary lossless compression (for a discrete source, $R(0)=H(X)$, matching [[4. Source Coding]]), and $R(D)$ is non-increasing in $D$ — tolerating more distortion can only reduce the required rate, never increase it.

### Example: The Gaussian Source

- For $X\sim N(0,\sigma^2)$ with squared-error distortion, the rate-distortion function is
	$$
	R(D) = \begin{cases}\dfrac12\log\dfrac{\sigma^2}{D} & 0\le D\le\sigma^2 \\[4pt] 0 & D>\sigma^2\end{cases}
	$$
- Intuition for the two cases: if $D\ge\sigma^2$, the reconstruction can simply ignore the source and always output $\hat X=0$ (the mean), achieving exactly $E[(X-0)^2] = \sigma^2 \le D$ at rate $0$ — no information needs to be sent at all. For $D<\sigma^2$, one can show (via the same convex-optimization machinery as channel capacity, omitted here) that the optimal test channel is the "reverse" of a Gaussian channel: $X = \hat X+Z$ with $\hat X\sim N(0,\sigma^2-D)$ and independent noise $Z\sim N(0,D)$, giving $I(X;\hat X) = h(X)-h(X\mid\hat X) = h(X)-h(Z) = \frac12\log(2\pi e\sigma^2) - \frac12\log(2\pi e D) = \frac12\log\frac{\sigma^2}{D}$.
- The formula is a mirror image of the Gaussian channel capacity $\frac12\log(1+P/N)$ from [[6. Differential Entropy and Gaussian Channels]]: both are "half-log of a ratio of variances," one arising from maximizing information subject to a power budget, the other from minimizing information subject to a distortion budget.
